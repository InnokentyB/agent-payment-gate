THE SOLUTION
A verified person. A narrow permission. Real settlement.
Didit verification links a person to signed spending consent. OpenZeppelin policies enforce one merchant, 2 XLM per payment and a 5 XLM rolling budget. The owner can revoke access.
Proven on testnet: payments, refusals, invoice reuse and revocation.

PMF
A wallet key cannot express what a human allowed.
Agent-commerce builders need bounded spending and clear receipts. Owners need control over the recipient, amount and expiry. Our first use case: agents purchasing paid APIs or merchant services.
Working prototype; customer discovery is next. No traction claimed.

TECHNICAL WORKFLOW
Verify → sign consent → enforce policies → pay → revoke.
Didit → signed wallet binding → OpenZeppelin smart account. Soroban policies restrict recipient, per-payment amount and budget. SQLite preserves invoice receipts; unknown results use the original hash.
